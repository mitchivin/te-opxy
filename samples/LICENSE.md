# Sample library provenance

The full GeneralUser GS v1.471 license is retained alongside the distributed
samples in [GeneralUser-LICENSE.txt](GeneralUser-LICENSE.txt). GeneralUser
instrument renders below retain that source license.

## Drum-machine single hits for the iconic excerpts

`instruments/drum-machines/*.wav` are dry individual hits rendered from
GeneralUser GS v1.471 with `node scripts/render-song-samples.mjs iconic-kit`.
The 808/909 kit (zero-based program 25) supplies key 36 (TR-909 kick), key 40
(TR-909 snare), key 35 (TR-808 kick), and key 38 (TR-808 snare). The sample
names are present in the SoundFont's sample table. Electronic kit program 24
supplies key 38 (snare) and 41 (low tom); Standard program 0 supplies keys
42/46 (acoustic closed/open hi-hat). Dance program 26 supplies keys 42/46
(TR-909 closed/open hi-hat), used with native mute groups for the dance tracks.
Each file is peak-normalized independently with
no effects, phrase, pattern or reference-song audio. See the GeneralUser
license in `node_modules/generaluser/LICENSE.txt`. This does not identify
these samples as the exact processed hits in any original recording.


## Acoustic piano for instrumental reconstructions

`instruments/acoustic-piano/piano-*.wav` are individual MIDI notes 54, 58,
62, 66, 70, 74, 78, 82 and 86 from GeneralUser GS v1.471, program 1 (Bright Acoustic Piano),
rendered without effects by `node scripts/render-song-samples.mjs piano`
using the declared `generaluser` and `spessasynth_core` dependencies.
GeneralUser permits redistribution and use in software and musical works;
its license is included at `node_modules/generaluser/LICENSE.txt`.
`field-piano-as4.wav` is the same single MIDI 70 note repacked by
`python scripts/op1-field/prepare-catalogue.py` for the Field sampler, with a
1100 Hz high-pass and 932 Hz / Q 2.5 / +3 dB EQ, then peak-normalized to 0.89.
This is a processed licensed note, not a sample from Halloween.
These files contain no reference recording, musical phrase or song stem.

`instruments/overdrive-guitar/guitar-*.wav` are six individual MIDI notes
42, 46, 50, 54, 58 and 62, rendered from the same GeneralUser GS SoundFont,
program 29 (Overdriven Guitar), without effects. Reproduce them with
`node scripts/render-song-samples.mjs iconic-guitar`. The same GeneralUser
license applies. Power chords and their rhythm are played by the XY sampler;
no guitar phrase or backing track is included.

`instruments/plucked-guitar/guitar-*.wav` are seven individual MIDI notes
46, 49, 53, 56, 60, 63 and 67 from the same GeneralUser GS v1.471 library,
zero-based program 24 (Acoustic Guitar, nylon). Reproduce with
`node scripts/render-song-samples.mjs iconic-plucked-guitar`. Each is a dry
single key held for two seconds with a .6-second release tail, independently
peak-normalized and supplied with MIDI root metadata. The same GeneralUser
license applies. All figure timing and delay are played by the XY device;
the files contain no Robert Miles recording, stem or arranged phrase.

The seven single-note and single-hit WAV files under `demo/` are generated
entirely by `scripts/generate-sampler-fixtures.py` in this repository. Those
seven files use no source recording, manufacturer sound library, musical
phrase, or third-party sample.

`demo/drums/field-kit.wav` is a stereo packing of the same four CC0 demo hits,
generated by `scripts/op1-field/generate-demo-drum-kit.py`. It contains 24
equal regions in one sample, repeating the four hits; it contains no Freestyle
recording or song rhythm. It is a functional DRUM sampler preset and is not a
selected Don't Stop the Rock palette. It is also dedicated to CC0 1.0.

The generated audio is dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0:
https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/

## Don't Stop the Rock Field palette

`songs/dont-stop-the-rock/lead-g5.wav` and `bass-f2.wav` are individual
notes, rendered without effects from GeneralUser GS v1.471 (program 90
Polysynth, MIDI 79; program 87 Bass & Lead, MIDI 41) by
`scripts/op1-field/render-rock-candidates.mjs`. GeneralUser GS permits use in
software and music projects; see its bundled `node_modules/generaluser/LICENSE.txt`.
Neither file contains a phrase, stem, or audio from the Freestyle recording.

`songs/dont-stop-the-rock/drum-kit.wav` packs 24 equal, editable stereo
regions from four CC0 Sonic Pi hits using
`scripts/op1-field/generate-rock-kit.py`: `bd_808` (EKVelika, Freesound
208447), `sn_generic` (hullum, 415582), `hat_tap` (TheEndOfACycle,
674296), and `perc_snap` (SoundCollectah, 109400). The sequence of drum
hits is performed by the Field song score, not embedded in the sample.

22,050 Hz, 16-bit mono. Pitched roots: reed/pluck C4 (MIDI 60), bass C2
(MIDI 36). These are functional demo sounds, not a selected 19-2000 palette.

The air-key-c5 and finger-bass-c2 single notes are also original CC0 assets, reproducible with scripts/generate-song-notes.py. They contain no recording, riff or stem from Gorillaz or Teenage Engineering. They are available to every sampler track through the studio folder.

The four `songs/mario/voices/*.wav` files are original CC0 oscillator and
noise sounds generated by `scripts/generate-mario-voices.py`. They contain no
Nintendo recording or sequence. The song's notes are entered by the device
build and remain editable in the project.

`noise-short.wav`, `noise-strong.wav` and `noise-long.wav` in that folder are
additional original CC0 single hits generated by `scripts/generate-mario-noise.py`.
They independently implement the documented NES long-mode LFSR and the ground
theme's noise register settings: timer periods 32/762/32 CPU cycles and length
counters 2/2/10 half-frame ticks. Approximate 90 Hz / 440 Hz high-pass and 14 kHz
low-pass output filters are applied at eight-times oversampling. No ROM,
reference audio, notes, riff or musical phrase is read by the generator.

`songs/project-lee/oscillators/portamento-lead-gs5.wav` is an original CC0 oscillator single note,
generated by `scripts/generate-project-lee-lead.py`. It contains no pitch
sequence, transition or reference audio; the device applies all notes and
portamento during playback.

`songs/project-lee/oscillators/rolling-bass-gs1.wav` is an original CC0 oscillator single note (root
G#1), generated by `scripts/generate-project-lee-rhythm.py`. It contains no
audio from DJ Settler's recording, no phrase, and no backing loop.

### Field catalogue derivatives

The WAV files in `songs/clint-eastwood/field/`, `songs/still-dre/field/`,
`songs/numb-encore/field/` and `songs/mario/field/` are prepared derivatives
of the individual licensed sources already attributed above. Exact source
paths and operations are listed in `src/op1-field/song-sample-manifest.json`.
Clint uses GeneralUser GS Electric Grand F#3 and Picked Bass G1; Still D.R.E.
uses GeneralUser GS Electric Grand E5, Bass & Lead E1 and Strings Fast C5;
`songs/still-dre/field/strings-c5.wav` is an unchanged copy of the dry
`instruments/strings/strings-c5.wav` single key. Attack trimming is performed
with the editable sampler Start control. +52-cent tuning
is now applied by the editable Field sampler rather than baked into the files;
Numb uses GeneralUser GS Polysynth A#5 / A#4 and Synth Bass 1 A#2. The Mario
pulse and triangle notes extend whole cycles of this project's original
chip-style single-note WAVs; its noise hit is likewise an original generated
source. These are not samples of the Nintendo recording.

Each drum kit concatenates its song's existing GeneralUser GS / Sonic Pi CC0
hits (DRE uses the shared GeneralUser Power snare), or Mario's original noise
hit, into 24 editable regions. The GeneralUser permissions and Sonic Pi CC0
attributions above apply unchanged. Preparation includes mono conversion,
optional filtering, headroom and region-end fades. Reproduce with
`python scripts/op1-field/prepare-catalogue.py` (ffmpeg required).
No recorded song, rendered OP-XY performance or musical phrase is included.

### Independent FM brass instrument

`instruments/fm-brass/brass-*.wav` contains six fresh, dry single-note renders
of Yamaha's published DX7 BRASS1 programming example (owner manual, printed
p.28), with a separately programmed +18-semitone/10 ms pitch attack inspired
by Audible Genius/Syntorial's independent by-ear lead recreation:
https://www.syntorial.com/preset-recipe/final-countdown-europe-lead/ . This
attack is an adaptation, not Yamaha's published neutral pitch envelope or
an identified original recording setting. No Syntorial audio or preset file
is used. The numeric patch and distinction are documented in
`docs/fm-brass-instrument.md`.
The renderer adapts Google Inc.'s Music Synthesizer for Android (2012/2013),
licensed under Apache 2.0; its complete license and modification notice are in
`third-party/music-synthesizer-for-android/`. Reproduce with
`node scripts/render-fm-brass-samples.mjs`. No Yamaha ROM audio, song recording
or musical phrase is included. BRASS1 is not identified as the original
Final Countdown TX816 patch.

### Independent Veridis FM instrument keys

`instruments/veridis-fm/flute-c5.wav` and `pipes-c4.wav` are independent dry
5.9-second mono keys synthesized by this project's floating-point renderer.
Numeric Yamaha DX7 factory programs ROM2A02 FLUTE2 and ROM1B17 PIPES2 were
read from the [Yamaha Black Boxes program archive](https://yamahablackboxes.com/collection/yamaha-dx7-synthesizer/patches/).
These contain newly synthesized audio, not Yamaha ROM waveforms or audio
from a song. PIPES2's carriers sound one octave above its played key; its
WAV root therefore labels the resulting C4, not the played C3.

The renderer adapts Google Music Synthesizer for Android envelope/scaling
code under Apache2.0; see the complete license and modification NOTICE in
`third-party/music-synthesizer-for-android/`. Generate with
`node scripts/render-veridis-instruments.mjs`; complete parameters and
provenance are beside the WAVs. Carr used these programs in his independent
remake; the original session's exact instruments and settings remain
unestablished. No baked chord, octave layer, effect, arranged phrase or
original-song audio is present in either key.

### Hardware-compatible browser sources (2026-10-12)

The playable OP-XY library now uses PCM WAV files with MIDI root metadata.
Some WAVs preserve the decoded audio of earlier MP3 sources; conversion does
not restore lost compression detail. Those original MP3 files are retained in
scripts/source-samples outside the public library. All source credits and
permissions above continue to apply. No musical phrases were added.
